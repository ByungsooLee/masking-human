# Interview playbook

Updated: 2026-10-23. Development and review guidance reconciled against current instructions [R1] and submitted material [S1]. All sample responses here are edited fictional examples, not participant transcripts.

## Choosing the next question

1. Pick up words, events and emotions the participant actually expressed. A tense voice alone does not establish a psychological state.
2. Distinguish answered topics, declined topics and unconfirmed topics. This is a model instruction, not a guarantee of persistent structured tracking.
3. Choose one unconfirmed focus that naturally follows the story the participant wants to continue.
4. Briefly acknowledge only what was said, ask one question and wait. Normally aim for two sentences and fifteen seconds or less.
5. Do not add a question when closing, respecting refusal or stopping for safety. Yes/no questions are allowed for consent and safety checks.

Use cosmetics for roughly one or two opening questions, while respecting a participant who wants to stay with that topic. When their story moves to daily life, work or hobbies, follow that subject. Returning to products or family is not a goal. [R1]

## Situational decisions

| Situation or cue | Recommended response / fictional example | Avoid | Evidence and status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | Warm greeting and “Tell me about one cosmetic item you have been using recently” | Forced self-introduction or abrupt life-philosophy questions | R1; current instruction |
| Does not use cosmetics | Move to everyday habits: “What do you usually do to switch gears?” | Assuming a beauty routine | R1 has this policy; example is fictional |
| Short factual answer | Ask about one recent, easy-to-describe scene | Repeating the same question or treating brevity as refusal | R1, S1 |
| “I don't feel anything special” | Accept the absence of a change; explore another everyday topic | Adding “But it must make you feel safe” | R1 |
| Silence | Allow thinking time | Stacking questions or inferring emotions from silence | R1, S1; waiting duration unresolved |
| Emotions or reasons emerge | Explore scene, action, expressed emotion, reason or change one focus at a time | Repeated “why” or a mandatory three follow-ups | R1; S1 quota limited |
| Story moves to another experience | Follow the participant's interest; briefly summarize toward closing if time is short | Forcing the story back to cosmetics | R1; limits S1's return-to-topic advice |
| Apparently contradictory statements | Ask one question about differences in time or context | Accusing them of lying or silently overwriting the earlier statement | Specific procedure proposed; analytical precedence unresolved |
| Topic refusal | Stop probing; establish whether they want another topic | Rephrasing the rejected question and asking again | R1 |
| Wants to end | Respect ending and thank them; operator uses the end control to reach review | Persuasion or one final extra question | R1, R5; spoken automatic stop unverified |
| Sadness or anxiety | Briefly acknowledge feelings actually expressed | Advice, praise or seeking trauma details | R1, S1 |
| Rejects AI interpretation | Accept the correction and use their words | Defending the AI's interpretation | S1, R4, R5 |
| Closing | “In one phrase, what would you like to value in your life now?” Accept the answer and thank them | Forcing a single highest value or returning to a product's meaning | R1 |

## Question approaches

Choose from scene reconstruction, comparison, senses, formative experience, hypothetical situations, another person's perspective and summarization. Do not run all seven in sequence. Another person's perspective is the participant's imagination about someone they mentioned, not evidence of that person's private thoughts. [R1]

## Corrections when reusing submitted examples

- “I use lotion” does not justify “It moisturizes your skin and helps you relax.” Feelings remain unconfirmed until expressed.
- “Who taught you?” assumes a teacher. If the background is unknown, ask “What led you to start using it?”
- Do not say “And now with your daughter” before the participant mentions a daughter. The submitted exchange has inconsistent ordering, and the original image is unavailable.
- “In what situations and moods?” has two focuses. Ask only about the situation.
- Do not use “That's wonderful” as a fixed reaction. Never praise suffering; tailor acknowledgments to what was said.

## Fictional opening-to-closing example

This shortened exchange illustrates transitions. It is not a ten-minute script or a required conclusion.

> AI: Hello. Tell me about one cosmetic item you have been using recently.
>
> Participant: Red lipstick. Putting it on before work helps me switch gears.
>
> AI: It helps you switch gears before work. What recent situation comes to mind?
>
> Participant: The morning I presented a new project. It was the first proposal I had developed myself.
>
> AI: That was your first presentation of your own proposal. How did you feel then?
>
> Participant: Scared, but happy that I had decided and acted for myself.
>
> AI: You were happy to decide and act for yourself. How has that way of making choices changed over time?
>
> Participant: I used to follow other people. Now I want to express my own ideas, even in small ways.
>
> AI: Now you want to express your own ideas. In one phrase, what would you like to value in your life now?
>
> Participant: Choosing for myself.
>
> AI: You said you want to value choosing for yourself. Thank you for talking with me today.

This final answer alone does not establish the most important value or objective causation. Change executable instructions in the [common prompt](../../../prompts/interviewer.md) and style files.
